# IB DP Extended Essay Criteria — Physics

## Source and currency
- Model: *Extended essay guide*, first assessment 2027 (published February 2025). Five criteria,
  30 marks. It replaces the 34-mark model (Focus and method, Knowledge and understanding, Critical
  thinking, Presentation, Engagement) used up to May 2026.
- **Status: paraphrased.** The criterion names, maximum marks and markband structure match the
  published model. The descriptor wording below is a close paraphrase assembled from secondary
  summaries of the guide, not the official text. Replace each descriptor with the verbatim guide
  text (with page numbers) before relying on the marks.
- The same criteria apply to every subject. This file adds physics-specific application notes,
  labelled as app guidance.

## Application notes
These notes explain how the app applies the criteria; they do not replace or add criteria.

- Use a holistic, best-fit judgment within each criterion. An essay may show qualities from
  adjacent bands; award the mark that most fairly reflects the evidence as a whole.
- Criteria A–D assess the essay only. Criterion E assesses only the reflective statement on the
  Reflection and Progress Form (RPF). Do not credit RPF content under A–D, or essay content under E.
- The essay has a 4,000-word limit (RPF reflective statement: 500 words). Examiners do not read or
  assess material beyond the limit. The app cannot count words reliably; if the essay looks
  substantially over the limit, say so and recommend human review instead of adjusting marks.
- Do not reward or penalize features that are not part of the criterion being assessed.

### Physics application notes (app guidance)
- A physics EE answers a research question in physics through focused, evidence-based argument
  that applies the principles of physics. Evidence may come from the student's own experiment,
  simulation or computation, from secondary data (databases, published data sets), or from
  literature- and theory-based research. No single method is required; secondary-data and
  theoretical essays can reach the top bands.
- The intended reader is a peer familiar with DP Physics. Standard course physics need not be
  derived; physics beyond the course should be explained and referenced.
- Where data are used, consider their quality: uncertainties, the reliability of secondary
  sources and experimental design, and whether the processing supports the argument. Do not
  impose a universal number of trials, data points or calculations.
- Experimental essays are not marked as an IA. Methods matter under A, analysis under C, and
  discussion and evaluation under D, judged against the EE descriptors.

## Criteria overview
- A. Framework for the essay (max: 6)
- B. Knowledge and understanding (max: 6)
- C. Analysis and line of argument (max: 6)
- D. Discussion and evaluation (max: 8)
- E. Reflection (max: 4) — assessed from the RPF reflective statement

## Criterion details

### Framework for the essay (max: 6)
Does the research question, together with the research methods and the structural conventions
used, give the essay an effective framework?

Strands: research question; research methods; structure.

| Markband | Descriptor (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The work does not reach the standard described by the descriptors below. |
| 1–2 | The research question is stated but is unclear, too broad or only loosely related to the topic. Research methods are stated, with limited suitability for the question or limited application. Structural conventions are used inconsistently and give little support to communicating the research. |
| 3–4 | The research question is relevant and clear but not fully focused for the scope of the essay. Research methods are described and are generally suitable, but are applied only partly effectively. Structural conventions generally support communication of the research. |
| 5–6 | The research question is relevant to the topic, clear, and focused for the scope of the essay. Research methods suitable for the question are explained and applied effectively. Structural conventions support communication of the research. |

### Knowledge and understanding (max: 6)
Does the student demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the subject matter used in the
research?

Strands: knowledge (including selection and use of sources); understanding — terminology;
understanding — concepts.

| Markband | Descriptor (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The work does not reach the standard described by the descriptors below. |
| 1–2 | Sources have limited relevance or are only partly appropriate to the research question, and show limited knowledge of the subject matter. Subject terminology and concepts are used insufficiently or inaccurately. |
| 3–4 | Relevant sources are used and demonstrate some knowledge of the subject matter and its academic context. Some relevant terminology and concepts are used accurately, though not consistently. |
| 5–6 | Relevant, appropriate sources are used effectively and demonstrate clear knowledge of the subject matter, placed in its wider academic context. Terminology and concepts are used accurately and consistently, showing understanding. |

### Analysis and line of argument (max: 6)
Does the student analyse the information presented in the essay and produce a line of
argument?

Strands: analysis; line of argument.

| Markband | Descriptor (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The work does not reach the standard described by the descriptors below. |
| 1–2 | The essay is mostly descriptive, with limited analysis and few findings relevant to the research question. The line of argument is partial or unclear. |
| 3–4 | The essay includes some analysis, which is only partly effective and produces some findings relevant to the research question. A somewhat consistent line of argument connects the research question, the findings and the conclusion. |
| 5–6 | The analysis is effective and produces findings relevant to the research question. A clear, consistent and coherent line of argument connects the research question, the findings and the conclusion. |

### Discussion and evaluation (max: 8)
Does the student discuss the findings and evaluate the essay?

Strands: discussion of the significance of the findings, supported by evidence; evaluation of
the research (its strengths and limitations).

| Markband | Descriptor (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The work does not reach the standard described by the descriptors below. |
| 1–2 | The essay describes the significance of the findings with little supporting evidence. Evaluation is minimal or partial, and strengths and limitations are not identified. |
| 3–4 | The essay includes a somewhat balanced discussion of the significance of the findings, sometimes supported by suitable evidence. The essay evaluates the research, stating some strengths and limitations. |
| 5–6 | The essay includes a balanced discussion of the significance of the findings, often supported by suitable evidence. The evaluation describes relevant strengths and limitations of the research. |
| 7–8 | The essay includes a balanced discussion of the significance of the findings, fully supported by suitable evidence. The evaluation is effective and explains the relevant strengths and limitations of the research and their effect on the conclusion. |

### Reflection (max: 4)
Does the student's reflective statement on the RPF evaluate the learning experience?

Assessed only from the RPF reflective statement (no more than 500 words), written after the
final reflection session (viva voce).

| Markband | Descriptor (paraphrased) |
|---|---|
| 0 | The work does not reach the standard described by the descriptors below, or no RPF reflective statement is available. |
| 1–2 | The reflective statement is mainly descriptive of the research process, with few or general examples and limited evidence of the student's growth as a learner. |
| 3–4 | The reflective statement is evaluative, includes specific examples, and shows how the experience developed the student as a learner and thinker, including skills or learning that can transfer to other contexts. |
